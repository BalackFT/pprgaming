**The votes are in, and it wasn't close.** Rockstar Games' UK employees have voted against a union recognition deal with the **IWGB Rockstar Game Workers Union**. The result was announced Thursday afternoon: **75.3% against, 24.7% in favor**, on a **93.2% turnout**, [The Independent reports](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/business/rockstar-iwgb-edinburgh-glasgow-b3092300.html). [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/grand-theft-auto/following-gta-6-release-rockstar-workers-vote-against-union-recognition-deal-but-those-in-favor-vow-to-keep-fighting/) puts the raw count at 1,163 votes against and 381 for.

We [covered the vote when it was announced](/news/rockstar-uk-union-recognition-vote-november-12-26-gta-vi/) back in October. The ballot ran from November 12 to 26, which put GTA VI's November 19 launch right in the middle of it. It covered staff at Rockstar's UK studios in Edinburgh, Leeds, Lincoln, Dundee and London, per [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/rockstar-game-workers-union-vote-gta-6/), and it followed about four months of talks between management and the union.

## What a "no" actually means

This was a vote on **recognition**, not a vote to shut the union down. As [Vertical Slice](https://www.verticalslice.news/lets-break-down-the-rockstar-union-recognition-vote-using-government-statistics/) explains, the union still exists. The IWGB says the deal would have given workers a formal way to bargain with management over pay, overtime, bonuses, leave and pensions, plus a right to be consulted on big changes like AI and redundancies. Without it, Rockstar isn't required to hold regular meetings with union reps. Staff can still join the IWGB and use their individual union rights.

## Both sides weigh in

Rockstar head of publishing **Jennifer Kolbe** said the result "reflects our team's strong support for continuing the direct relationships, collaboration and open dialogue" at the studio. The union isn't backing off. "This isn't a sign to retreat, it's a sign to double down on what we stand for," it said, adding that it has "more work to do" to win over colleagues. It says it will keep pushing for pay transparency, contractual overtime pay, and protections around AI and layoffs.

Polygon also notes that the two-week ballot overlapped with an employment tribunal hearing over Rockstar's firing of 31 workers in 2025.

**PPR take:** A 93% turnout means just about everybody showed up, and three out of four said no. That's a clear answer, and it deserves respect either way you lean. Still, timing matters. This vote ran during the biggest launch week the studio has ever had, when people are riding high and the paychecks look good. Whether that helped the "no" side, nobody can prove. The more interesting part is what comes next. The union says it represents hundreds of workers and plans to keep organizing, and the tribunal over the 2025 firings still hasn't ruled. So this isn't the end of the labor story at Rockstar. It's the end of round one. We'll keep an eye on it.
