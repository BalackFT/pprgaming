**Liquid Swords, the Swedish studio behind *Samson*, has gone bankrupt** just six months after its first game came out. Now the court-appointed trustee is trying to sell the studio's game before the week is over.

**What happened:** The Stockholm District Court declared Liquid Swords AB bankrupt on **October 12, 2026**, and named lawyer Karl Björlin of Cirio as trustee, according to [Cirio's official notice](https://cirio.se/news/liquidswords). Swedish outlet Breakit reports that the roughly 30 staff had gone **unpaid for at least two months**, and that the employees themselves asked for the bankruptcy, [PC Gamer reports](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/liquid-swords-developer-of-open-city-crime-sim-samson-has-gone-bankrupt-following-staff-claims-that-they-havent-been-paid-in-two-months/). Their unpaid wages will now be covered by Sweden's state wage guarantee, [Eurogamer notes](https://www.eurogamer.net/liquid-swords-samson-developer-bankrupt).

**Samson is for sale.** The trustee's [preliminary bidding documents](https://cirio.se/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/PRELIMINARY-BIDDING-DOCUMENTS-002.pdf) are open to anyone and give some unusually specific numbers:

- **Bids are due Wednesday.** Written indicative bids are due by **October 21 at 12:00 CEST** (6 AM ET). The estate can also sell before then.
- **What's included:** the Samson IP, source code, build tools, art, audio and documentation, plus trademarks such as "Liquid Swords," "Criminal Justice" and "Tyndleston," and domains such as thesamson.com.
- **There's a catch.** NetEase holds security over many of the Samson assets, so a buyer will have to deal with NetEase. The Unreal Engine license isn't part of the sale, and the **27 employees** don't transfer automatically.
- **Sales so far:** Samson has sold about **151,900 copies** on PlayStation, Xbox and Steam, for net revenue of about **SEK 24.1 million**, as of October 14.
- **Support continues for now.** The estate says it will try to keep Samson's support, patches and sales running during the bidding period.

**How it got here:** Liquid Swords was founded in 2020 by **Christofer Sundberg**, who co-founded Just Cause developer Avalanche Studios. *Samson: A Tyndalston Story* launched on PC on April 8 and on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S on September 21. PC Gamer says the studio had trouble finding a publisher, cut half its team last year and reworked the game into a run-based open-city crime game. It launched to mixed reviews and complaints about bugs and performance, and the studio was still patching it as recently as October 8.

**PPR take:** It's a rough way for a team to go out. They shipped their game, kept fixing it, and then went months without pay. The bidding documents are an unusually honest look at how little a mid-sized, mixed-reception action game can earn. We hope whoever picks up Samson brings at least some of those 27 developers along with it.
