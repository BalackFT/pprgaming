**Bandai Namco is closing Reflector Entertainment.** In a [notice to investors](https://www.bandainamco.co.jp/files/E_HP_Clean__E980A3E7B590E5AD90E4BC9AE7A4BEE381AEE8.pdf), Bandai Namco Holdings says its board passed a resolution to "discontinue Reflector's business, dissolve and liquidate it" after weighing "future business plans, necessary additional investment, and the possibility of profitability and business continuation." Reflector is the Montreal studio behind Unknown 9: Awakening, and that turned out to be the only game it ever shipped.

Bandai bought Reflector in September 2020, when the studio was already building Unknown 9 as a full multimedia franchise with a novel, comics and a podcast alongside the game. [Gematsu notes](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/11/bandai-namco-to-shut-down-reflector-entertainment) the studio was also supposed to work on existing Bandai Namco properties, but those plans never came to anything. Unknown 9: Awakening, a supernatural stealth game starring Anya Chalotra from The Witcher, launched on October 18, 2024, and landed with a 59 Metascore, [Polygon reports](https://www.polygon.com/bandai-namco-reflector-entertainment-shutdown-unknown-9-awakening/).

The fallout started fast. About 18% of the staff were laid off a month after launch, and in early 2025 more people were let go and a follow-up project was cancelled. CEO Hervé Hoerdt said back then that the decision "correlates directly with the failure of the studio's ambitious and courageous first project." Even so, [Insider Gaming points out](https://insider-gaming.com/bandai-namco-announces-reflector-entertainment-liquidation/) that Reflector's own website still teases a new game it can't talk about yet.

The money side is blunt. Bandai expects to book an extraordinary loss of about €21 million (roughly ¥3.5 billion) in the third quarter of the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, and says the final amount and timing are still under review. There's no firm date for the shutdown either. The notice says liquidation will wrap once the required local legal steps are done. Bandai hasn't said how many people still work at Reflector.

**PPR take:** This one feels like it was coming for two years, and that doesn't make it hurt less. Reflector got bought before it shipped anything, bet everything on a brand-new IP with books and comics and a podcast attached, and then the game didn't connect. One launch, two rounds of layoffs, a cancelled follow-up, and now the doors close. That's a brutal arc for a studio that only got one real shot. The part that gets me is the website still teasing a new project. Somebody in Montreal was working on that this week. Bandai is having a strong year with Ace Combat 8 and The Blood of Dawnwalker, which shows the publisher can afford to be patient when it wants to be. It just wasn't willing to here. Our thoughts are with everyone at Reflector. We hope their next studio gives them the second swing this one never got.
