**Fairgames is done.** Sony has stopped development on the PvP heist shooter from its Haven Studios subsidiary, [Bloomberg reported](https://www.theverge.com/games/1031242/sony-playstation-fairgames-canceled) today. The game was first revealed in May 2023 as *Fairgame$* and was planned for PS5 and PC.

According to an internal email from Sony Interactive Entertainment executives Sean Decker and John Doyle, which Bloomberg reviewed, a recent playtest with thousands of players didn't hit the mark. "While the test demonstrated strong operational excellence, live ops readiness, and community engagement, player feedback and retention results were mixed and fell short of the goals we had set," the memo reads, [per Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/11/fairgame-canceled). It goes on to cite "challenging market conditions" in the PvP shooter genre as part of the decision.

The good news, if there is any: a Sony spokesperson told Bloomberg there will be **no job cuts** from the cancellation, and Haven will move to support other studios inside PlayStation. Haven isn't closing, [VGC notes](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/playstation-reportedly-cancels-fairgames-after-recent-playtests/).

Fairgames had a rough road. Sony bought Haven in 2022, and the game was pitched as a competitive heist shooter where crews break into exotic locations and steal from the rich while racing other teams for the loot. [Final Weapon reports](https://finalweapon.net/2026/11/10/fairgames-cancelled-by-playstation/) the project had been reworked several times and was due for a re-reveal under a new title, and Haven founder Jade Raymond left PlayStation last year. Footage that appeared to come from the playtest leaked in September, which was likely the first real look most people got at it.

It also fits a pattern. As [The Verge points out](https://www.theverge.com/games/1031242/sony-playstation-fairgames-canceled), Sony has already cancelled live-service projects tied to God of War and The Last of Us and shut down Concord shortly after launch. Helldivers 2 remains its clear hit, Bungie's Marathon has had a rough start (with a December update just shown off today), and a live-service Horizon spinoff was recently rebooted.

**PPR take:** Honestly, this one was a long time coming. Fairgames spent three years mostly as a CG trailer and a question mark, and a heist PvP shooter was always going to be a tough sell against what's already out there. The part I respect is the "no layoffs" line. Too many cancellations this year have come with a pink slip attached, so Haven staying open and moving its people onto other PlayStation projects is about the best version of bad news. The bigger takeaway for players is that Sony seems done chasing every live-service trend. Fewer forced multiplayer bets could mean more of the single-player games PlayStation is actually known for, and I'm fine with that. Still a bummer for the team that put years into it.
