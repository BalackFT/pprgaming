**Half a billion dollars.** That's what one source familiar with Xbox's business told [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-case-for-exclusivity-when-gears-of-war-e-day-cost-over-500-million-to-make-2000747986) **Gears of War: E-Day** cost to make, and that's before marketing. Microsoft didn't respond to Kotaku's request for comment.

**Why it matters:** E-Day has the best Metacritic score the series has seen since the original Xbox 360 trilogy, but the sales estimates are soft:

- **About 230,000 copies and $37 million** in its first week, according to Alinea Analytics, and a big chunk of that was Game Pass subscribers paying $30 for the Premium Edition early-access upgrade.
- **Fewer than 100,000 copies on Steam** so far, by the same estimates.
- **No PS5 version.** Xbox made E-Day a console exclusive earlier this year. Kotaku's math says matching Gears of War: Reloaded's estimated 684,000 PS5 sales would have added only about $33 million after Sony's cut.

According to Kotaku's source, E-Day was unlikely to earn back its cost on any combination of platforms, but as an exclusive it could help bring players back to Xbox.

**Not everyone buys the number.** Insider Gaming's Tom Henderson said back in June the game cost over $400 million. Windows Central's Jez Corden pushed back then, arguing The Coalition also builds Unreal Engine 5 tech for other Xbox teams, so not all of its spending belongs to E-Day ([Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2026/10/new-rumour-says-xbox-spent-usd500-million-on-gears-of-war-e-day-but-not-everyone-agrees) has the back-and-forth). Forbes' [Paul Tassi](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/gears-war-e-day-cost-140648153.html) calls the figure plausible, pointing to the roughly $315 million Spider-Man 2 cost according to the 2023 Insomniac leak.

**PPR take:** Whatever the exact figure, the gap between "great reviews" and "great business" has never looked wider. This lands a day after EA closed BioWare, and budgets like this are a big part of why studios keep getting hit.
