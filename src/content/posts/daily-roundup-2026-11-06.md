Happy Friday! Three more stories that didn't get their own full write-up today, but they're still worth catching.

<h2 id="fenris-eve-frontier-layoffs">Fenris Creations lays off an undisclosed number from the Eve Frontier team</h2>

Eve Online and Eve Frontier developer Fenris Creations has confirmed layoffs on the Eve Frontier team. Confirming the cuts to Vertical Slice, as [This Week in Videogames](https://thisweekinvideogames.com/news/fenris-creations-confirms-undisclosed-number-of-layoffs-on-eve-frontier-team/) reports, the studio said the changes follow "a review of the project's priorities, structure, and development plans" and that Eve Frontier "remains in active development." LinkedIn posts point to QA, art, and engineering among the roles hit. Headcount wasn't disclosed. The timing is rough: it's about six months after Pearl Abyss sold Fenris (then CCP) back to management in a roughly $120 million deal in May 2026. The studio rebranded to Fenris Creations and had assured at the time that the transition involved no restructuring or layoffs. Google also took a minority stake and struck a DeepMind research partnership using an offline Eve Online environment. That assurance didn't hold for Eve Frontier's team.

<h2 id="mob-entertainment-escape-from-playtime-layoffs">Mob Entertainment cuts 16 from the Escape From Playtime team</h2>

On November 4, Mob Entertainment laid off 16 people from the Escape From Playtime team, [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/escape-from-playtime-layoffs-mob-entertainment/) reports, "the minority of whom were permanent employees." Lead designer Richard Maddern posted on LinkedIn that he and others are looking for work. The studio says the game is still in active development through early access and that plans for a full 1.0 launch haven't changed, with roadmap tweaks already coming from community feedback. Priority right now, they say, is supporting the people affected. Escape From Playtime launched in early access last month and hasn't matched the pull of Mob's Poppy Playtime titles; we covered that [early-access launch and Chapter 6 teaser](/news/escape-from-playtime-early-access-poppy-playtime-chapter-6-teaser/) when it dropped. Sixteen people is a real hit for a live-service early-access team, even if most weren't permanent.

<h2 id="deca-acquires-island-hoppers">DECA Group acquires Island Hoppers</h2>

Embracer Group announced today that DECA Group, through wholly owned subsidiary A Thinking Ape Entertainment (ATA), has acquired the full assets and rights to the mobile game Island Hoppers from Nexters Global LTD (owned by GDEV Inc.). The [official press release](https://embracer.com/releases/deca-group-acquires-island-hoppers/) puts the upfront price at roughly USD 4.5 million, plus an earn-out of up to about USD 0.5 million, all cash, with an estimated payback of 2.5 to 3.5 years. Island Hoppers is a free-to-play casual farming and adventure title — islands, quests, puzzles, farming, estate restoration — on iOS, Android, and browser, launched in October 2023. ATA will run publishing and development, including user acquisition, live ops, and content. The deal actually closed in August 2026 and is being announced now because of earlier commercial restrictions. DECA CEO Ken Go framed it as investing in games that can live longer than the market expects: "Great games can have much longer lives than the market often expects."

**PPR take:** Two layoff stories and one small Embracer mobile buy on the same Friday. Fenris got sold back to management with a public "no layoffs" line six months ago, then quietly resized Eve Frontier anyway — that sting lands harder than a routine trim. Mob is still shipping Escape From Playtime toward 1.0 after a soft early-access run, which is better than killing the project, but sixteen people out the door is still sixteen people. Island Hoppers is the quieter contrast: a few million dollars for a live F2P title ATA thinks it can milk for years. Studios keep cutting to stay focused while the parent companies that still have cash scoop up anything with a sticky audience. Hope the folks looking for work land somewhere solid.
