**There was really only one name for this.** The Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences (AIAS) says Shigeru Miyamoto will be the first person to receive its new Legend Award, at a special 30th Annual D.I.C.E. Awards ceremony. The [AIAS announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/shigeru-miyamoto-critically-acclaimed-nintendo-director-and-fellow-selected-as-inaugural-legend-award-inductee-at-30th-annual-dice-awards-302940899.html) went out on November 16, and [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/console/shigeru-miyamoto-selected-as-inaugural-legend-award-inductee-at-d-i-c-e-awards) reported it the same day.

## What the Legend Award is

This isn't the Hall of Fame under a new name. The AIAS describes the Legend Award as recognition for "an industry icon whose enduring vision continues to redefine interactive entertainment," and calls it "a generational honor" that is handed out no more than once per decade. So whoever gets the next one, it probably won't be for a while.

AIAS President Meggan Scavio said Miyamoto "perfectly embodies" the award, and that the Academy is "humbled and excited" to have him accept it at the 30th anniversary show. The ceremony is set for Thursday, February 18, 2027 at 8:00 PM PT at the Cosmopolitan in Las Vegas, according to the release.

## Why him

The release runs through the résumé, and it's a long one. Miyamoto joined Nintendo in 1977 after studying industrial design at Kanazawa College of Art. In 1981 he made Donkey Kong, which the AIAS calls the first game created by an art designer. From there it's Super Mario Bros. and The Legend of Zelda, producing the Kirby and Pokémon series, and creating Pikmin, Wii Sports and Wii Fit. He's been Nintendo's Representative Director and Fellow since 2015, and these days the AIAS says he's focused on bringing Nintendo's worlds to theme parks and film.

**PPR take:** Some awards make you squint at the pick. This one doesn't. Pretty much everybody reading this site has played something Miyamoto touched, whether it was Donkey Kong in an arcade, Mario on a hand-me-down NES or Wii Sports bowling with the family at the holidays. What I like about the "once a decade" rule is that it keeps the award from turning into a yearly lifetime-achievement trophy that loses its meaning. Starting it with the guy who basically wrote the rules for how a game should feel in your hands sets the bar about as high as it can go. I'll be curious to see whether he shows up in Las Vegas in person, but the AIAS hasn't said, so we'll wait and see. Either way, well deserved.
