**Tripwire Interactive has laid off 36 people.** The studio behind Killing Floor and Maneater announced the cuts on November 9, calling the move "certainly not an easy decision, but unfortunately a necessary one for the organization," and saying departing staff will be supported through the transition. It didn't give a reason or say which teams were hit.

This is a big bite out of a mid-sized studio. A source at Tripwire [told PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/a-week-after-expanding-its-publishing-division-killing-floor-studio-tripwire-interactive-lays-off-36-people/) that roughly 40 to 50 employees are left after the layoffs. It's also the second round this year. Back in March, Tripwire cut 23 people in what it called a "workforce reduction" affecting "different areas," [Game Developer reported](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/embracer-subsidiary-tripwire-interactive-has-laid-off-23-employees) at the time. That brings 2026's publicly announced total to 59, as [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/tripwire-interactive-announces-layoffs/) tallied.

Tripwire says the work keeps going. "Our team is focused on our internal projects, including active development of Maneater 2, ongoing work on Killing Floor 3, and publishing efforts under the Tripwire Presents label," the studio wrote. Maneater 2 was announced in September, five years after the original shark RPG, and [Noisy Pixel notes](https://noisypixel.net/tripwire-interactive-layoffs-36-employees-killing-floor-3/) it's being built with Demiurge Studios and is due in 2027. Tripwire hasn't said whether the cuts change that timing or Killing Floor 3's update plans.

The timing stings. Less than a week ago, Tripwire expanded Tripwire Presents to offer "scaled publishing support" to smaller indie developers. Meanwhile Killing Floor 3, which launched in July 2025, still sits at "mixed" user reviews on Steam, with a concurrent player count PC Gamer describes as a small fraction of Killing Floor 2's. Tripwire has been owned by Embracer Group since 2022 and stayed with Embracer when Saber Interactive was sold off in 2024.

**PPR take:** Two rounds of layoffs in eight months, and the second one leaves a studio of maybe 40 to 50 people trying to ship a Maneater sequel, keep Killing Floor 3 alive and run a growing publishing label. That's a lot of plates for a team that just lost 36 people. Expanding Tripwire Presents one week and cutting staff the next is a rough look, even if those decisions come from different budgets. Killing Floor 3 never caught on the way KF2 did, and you can feel that pressure in every line of the statement. Maneater was a genuinely fun surprise, and we'd love to see the sequel land, but it now has to do that with a much thinner crew. Our thoughts are with everyone who got the call today. We hope they land somewhere good, fast.
