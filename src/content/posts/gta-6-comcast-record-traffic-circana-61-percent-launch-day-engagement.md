**GTA 6 didn't just sell. It clogged the pipes.** Four days after launch, the week-one picture is filling in, and two of the biggest numbers didn't come from Rockstar at all. One came from an internet provider, the other from the firm that tracks what people actually play.

## Comcast: the busiest day in its network's history

In a [post on November 20](https://corporate.comcast.com/stories/the-biggest-day-in-internet-history), Comcast said **November 18**, the last day of pre-loads, was the busiest day its network has ever had, with peak traffic **31% above an average day**. GameSpot [reports](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/gta-6-sets-even-more-records/) that was 9.6% above Comcast's previous single-day record. Launch day itself, November 19, landed at number two, with Thursday Night Football piled on top.

A few more from Comcast's [release](https://pubt.io/view/E0FFF80336FE5E7611E92B02FF654F86923EA90F):

- At peak, GTA VI was **as much as 24% of all traffic** on Comcast's network
- The launch period now owns **five of the network's 10 busiest days** ever
- **Twitch traffic jumped about 50%** as people tuned in to watch

That's one US provider, not "the internet." Comcast's numbers cover Comcast's network and nothing else.

## Circana: over half of active console players were in Leonida

On the play side, Circana analyst [Mat Piscatella](https://bsky.app/profile/matpiscatella.bsky.social) posted Player Engagement Tracker data on November 20. Of players active on November 19, **61% of US PS5 players** and **56% of Xbox players worldwide** "engaged with" GTA VI. PS5 players averaged **513 minutes** in the game that day, Xbox players **494**. He called both rates "absolutely bonkers" and said that, to his recollection, there has been "nothing near this" at Circana, per [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/gta-6-half-of-all-active-console-users-played/). Fortnite was a distant second at about 10%, per [PlayStation Universe](https://www.psu.com/news/grand-theft-auto-vi-launch-day-saw-over-half-of-all-active-console-users-playing-the-game-says-circana/).

Read that carefully. It's a share of people who turned their console on that day, not of everyone who owns one. And the PS5 number is US-only while Xbox is global, so they don't line up one-to-one.

## Where week one stands

The only official sales numbers are still Take-Two's: [**$3.79 billion** in day-one retail sales and **32.5 million** copies sold before launch](https://pprgaming.com/news/gta-6-3-79-billion-day-one-sales-32-5-million-pre-release-take-two/), both company estimates. Rockstar hasn't given a week-one sales or player count. This morning it pushed the game's [first post-launch patch](https://pprgaming.com/news/gta-6-first-post-launch-patch-01-002-002-ps5-xbox-no-patch-notes/), fixing autosave, stuck weather and a batch of crashes.

## PPR take

Eight and a half hours. On a Thursday. That's the stat that gets me, more than the billions. The money was always going to be silly, and Take-Two already banked it before anybody pressed start. But an average of 513 minutes on launch day is a full workday with the game running, for the average player, on a Thursday. That's not a launch, that's a holiday nobody declared.

The Comcast number is the fun one, though. A single game was close to a quarter of a major ISP's traffic at peak. That's what a download of up to 212GB times millions of pre-loads looks like. Next time someone tells you game sizes don't matter, point them here.

What I'd still like to see is a real week-one count from Rockstar. Until then, these are trackers and an ISP, and they're good ones, but they aren't sales. Either way, nobody is going to call this launch quiet.
