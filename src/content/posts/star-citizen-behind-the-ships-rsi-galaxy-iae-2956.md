**She's finally getting her close-up.** The official Star Citizen channel dropped a new [Behind the Ships: RSI Galaxy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1YzZqThl2k8) episode today (also listed on the [RSI Comm-Link](https://robertsspaceindustries.com/en/comm-link/transmission/21447-Star-Citizen-Behind-The-Ships-RSI-Galaxy)), and the YouTube blurb doesn't mince words: this long-awaited favorite is "lowering its landing gear" for a debut at **[IAE 2956](/news/star-citizen-iae-2956-official-november-13-27-new-babbage-free-fly/)**. [@RobertsSpaceInd](https://x.com/RobertsSpaceInd/status/2119777052408639882) welcomed her to the show floor the same morning. IAE doors open **November 13** at **Tobin Expo Center in New Babbage**, with Free Fly running through the 27th.

We already knew from the [October Monthly Report](/news/star-citizen-october-2026-monthly-report-300-contracts-nyx-i-merchantman/) that the Galaxy had hit final review and was picking up an updated RSI-branded UI. This episode is the tour — designers walking the chassis, the hangar, the modules, and what lands with her at IAE.

Watch it here: [Star Citizen | Behind the Ships: RSI Galaxy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1YzZqThl2k8) (about 9 minutes).

## Multi-role on purpose

The team frames the Galaxy the way RSI always sold her: a **large modular multi-role** ship built so a crew can stay out in the 'verse for extended runs and switch what she's doing without swapping hulls. She's a civilian ship, not a warship, but the episode still talks up meaningful armor and shielding, plus firepower the pilot and remote turret gunners can share — forward guns and full **360°** coverage once the turrets are manned.

On paper she's built for a **crew of six**. Bridge sits set back at the top of that familiar RSI arrowhead silhouette. Habitation covers a mess / relaxation space, individual crew rooms, and a dedicated captain's cabin. The designers say the interior layout moved a lot since whitebox so traversal feels cleaner and more welcoming, and she has **more windows than the original concept**, especially along the sides and upper decks.

## Hangar, turrets, and a real "pocket carrier" trick

The rear **XX-Small hangar** is still a headline feature. In the episode it comes with **repair, refuel, and rearm**, a door straight into that bay, and room to park a small utility ship for jobs like a quick salvage run before docking it again. Hangar cargo stays at **64 SCU** on grids that don't block the pad, so you can keep an XXS ship *and* that cargo at the same time.

Weapon layout from the video: the pilot owns the missiles, and there are **three remote turrets**, each with **two Size 5** mounts. Those turrets can be **slaved to the pilot**, so solo you can run all six Size 5s plus the missiles yourself. Once you've got crew, putting people on the stations is better — then you're covering multiple arcs instead of only what's forward. The team also moved the remote turret stations down to the front / lower deck; they used to sit up in the corridors, which felt wrong for a civilian hangout space.

And then there's the base module. It fills the mid-ship void without the fancy cargo or med gear, but if you can thread snubs through the rear hangar into that center section, the designers flat-out call her a **pocket carrier**. Even without a specialty module, they say she can still run a wide range of mission content.

## Cargo and medical at IAE — refinery and base-building later

**At launch / at IAE this year, two modules ship with her: cargo and medical.** Refinery and base-building modules are called out for later, once those gameplay systems are actually online in the PU.

The **cargo module** is the big number change from concept. Capacity went from the old **512 SCU** figure up to **656 SCU** inside the module. Add the hangar's 64 and you're looking at **720 SCU** total. Two **tractor beams** live in the module so you can sort containers while loading, and the rear hangar still lets a smaller cargo ship ferry boxes in and out without landing the Galaxy herself. The designers say they tried a bunch of bay layouts and settled on the one that fits a lot without making it impossible to actually move freight around.

The **medical module** also gained space from the interior redesign. The lift is more comfortable for bringing medical vehicles aboard for triage, and bed capacity is up. Per the episode: **three Tier 3, three Tier 2, and two Tier 1** beds, with storage for them. (Same idea as the old brochure pitch — dedicated med facility, not a tiny sickbay bolted on — just with more beds than the earlier layout.)

## RSI's year, and why this one hits different

The episode opens by stacking RSI's 2026 run: Hermes in January, Aurora Mk II and the Aurora Gold Standard, then the Constellation Mk V Centaurus and Skylark....and now the Galaxy. That tracks with what we've been watching all year. For a manufacturer week that opens IAE, putting the modular big sister of the Connie on the show floor is exactly the kind of drop the Tobin halls were built for.

**PPR take:** I've been waiting on this hull for a long time, and this BTS episode is the first time she feels *finished* instead of promised. Cargo at 720 with the hangar, a real med bay with Tier 1 through 3, XX-Small hangar that can repair/refuel/rearm, and that pocket-carrier base module trick....that's a lot of careers in one RSI chassis. Refinery and base-building still have to wait on their systems, and I'm fine with that if cargo and medical actually show up at New Babbage next week. Doors open the 13th.
