**Rough news out of Bit Reactor.** The studio behind Star Wars Zero Company laid off an unspecified number of staff on Friday, [Bespin Bulletin reports](https://bespinbulletin.com/star-wars-zero-company-developer-bit-reactor-hit-with-layoffs/). November 6 was the last day for the people named in that report. Bespin says it was first to the story; [Playground.ru](https://www.playground.ru/star_wars_zero_company/news/v_studii_bit_reactor_razrabotchike_star_wars_zero_company_proshli_sokrascheniya-1888547) has since carried the same outline. No official headcount has been published.

This lands after a hard few months for the team. In the weeks before launch, Bit Reactor ran out of money and put roughly **80% of staff on furlough**. In October the studio said **more than 50% of staff were returning to work**, and Patch 1.2 followed. [Kotaku covered that return](https://kotaku.com/star-wars-zero-company-studio-tries-to-bring-back-as-many-furloughed-devs-as-possible-following-unexpected-business-developments-outside-of-our-control-2000741943) when it happened. Since then, Bespin notes, virtually nothing has been shared about the game's future.

Sales are part of the picture Bespin lays out. Despite strong reviews, Zero Company has sold about **1 million copies**, short of what the studio needed. Not everyone came back off furlough onto full pay, so another round of cuts was always a risk.

Named staff who posted about being impacted, via LinkedIn as reported by Bespin, include Senior Environment Artist **Ryan Lopez** (nearly four years at the studio, key to The Den's exterior), UI Engineer **Tyler Eichten**, QA **Ryan Mosher**, and Producer **Michael Blackard**. Lopez wrote that despite the sting he was proud of the team's work and that "it was privilege to ship a game with you." Eichten said he was "immensely proud" of the UI/UX work and hoped coworkers (and himself) find placement. Blackard said he is "giddy at the idea of returning" to Bit Reactor someday.

**Update, November 7:** Lopez edited his LinkedIn post to clarify he was in the three-month furlough group, not the cohort brought back full-time. Per Bespin, that suggests returned full-time staff are not impacted as of now.

**PPR take:** This one stings because Zero Company actually got good reviews and still sold about a million copies, and that still wasn't enough runway for the whole team. We watched the pre-launch furlough, then the October return of more than half the studio, then Patch 1.2, and now Friday's cuts. Lopez clarifying he was in the furlough group, not the returned full-time cohort, is a small mercy for people who just came back, but it doesn't soften the hit for anyone whose last day was November 6. Thoughts are with Lopez, Eichten, Mosher, Blackard, and everyone else affected across art, UI, QA, and production. Hope they land somewhere that values the work they already shipped, and hope Bit Reactor can hold on for the people still there.
