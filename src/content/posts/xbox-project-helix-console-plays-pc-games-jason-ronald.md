**Microsoft finally drew a line on what its next Xbox actually is.** Project Helix is a console first. It just happens to be able to play games from your PC too. That's the word from Jason Ronald, Xbox's VP of Next Generation, in an interview printed in the Xbox 25th Anniversary Magazine and picked up by [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/xbox/microsoft-says-xbox-helix-is-a-console-not-a-pc-but-it-will-be-able-to-play-your-pc-games-helix-is-designed-to-be-a-console-that-can-also-play-games-from-your-pc) and [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/xbox-project-helix-is-a-console-that-plays-pc-games-not-the-other-way-around/) over the last couple of days.

## Console first, PC games second

Microsoft had already said Helix would play PC games, and plenty of folks took that to mean the next Xbox was really a Windows PC in a console shell. Ronald shut that down: "Helix is designed to be a console that is also capable of playing games from your PC. It is not a PC that can also play console games."

The pitch is that your whole library comes with you. Ronald says players will be able to bring "four generations" of their Xbox library and progression over, and that Microsoft wants to remove "the arbitrary constraints between console and PC games." For people who like to tinker, he says the deeper settings and performance options will be there if you want them. If you don't, the goal is "easy, intuitive, and instant."

## Its own interface, with lessons from the Ally

Helix won't just borrow the Xbox mode that ships on handhelds today. Ronald says its interface "will be its own," but feedback on the ROG Xbox Ally and Xbox mode "has fundamentally informed and altered" how it shows up on a future console. GameSpot also notes he described Helix as a brand that stretches across the living room, handhelds and cloud streaming, and that the next generation isn't about "8K, or 240 hertz" so much as gameplay ideas.

## What we still don't know

A lot. There's no price, though GameSpot says Ronald talked about aiming for an "affordable" one. And as Windows Central points out, "games from your PC" doesn't tell us how that works. Microsoft hasn't said which PC storefronts or launchers will be supported, or whether games run locally or stream from your own machine.

**PPR take:** I'll be honest, the console-first part is the part I like. Most people who buy a console want to sit down and play, not manage drivers and launcher updates, and Ronald seems to get that. The "four generations" promise is the real hook for longtime Xbox players, because a library that follows you is worth more than any spec sheet. BUT the PC side is still a big question mark. "Games from your PC" could mean anything from Steam running natively to streaming off the rig in your office, and those are very different machines. Until Microsoft answers that, and puts a price on it, Helix is a nice idea on paper. I'd keep the wallet closed and the eyebrows raised for now.
