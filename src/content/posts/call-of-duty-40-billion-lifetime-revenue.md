**$40 billion and counting.** Xbox CEO **Asha Sharma** says the **Call of Duty** franchise has now passed **$40 billion in lifetime revenue**. She shared the milestone on X on October 26, three days after **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4** launched, calling the new game "one of the most celebrated Call of Duty games in its 24-year history," [GameSpot reports](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/call-of-duty-lifetime-revenue-surges-past-40-billion-microsoft-confirms/). Her post came with a graphic of Modern Warfare 4's review scores.

**What the number does and doesn't cover.** The $40 billion is for the whole series since 2003, not Modern Warfare 4 alone, and Sharma didn't break it down or say when the franchise crossed the line, according to [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27729/call-of-duty-passes-40bn-in-lifetime-revenue-says-xbox-ceo-asha-sharma). It's revenue, not profit, and it includes years from before Microsoft bought Activision Blizzard in 2023. [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/call-of-duty/call-of-duty-passes-usd40-billion-in-lifetime-revenue-making-it-one-of-the-most-lucrative-videogame-franchise-ever/) notes that still leaves Call of Duty behind giants like Mario and Pokémon.

**No launch numbers yet.** Microsoft and Activision haven't shared any unit sales or launch revenue for Modern Warfare 4, a change from the days when Activision trumpeted launch records. Microsoft reports quarterly earnings on **Wednesday, October 28**, [Warp Pipes notes](https://warppipes.com/en/articles/video-game-news-digest-2026-10-27), so that's the next chance for real figures.

**PPR take:** A huge lifetime number is a nice headline, but when a publisher leads with franchise totals instead of launch sales, people notice. Tomorrow's earnings call should show whether Modern Warfare 4 is pulling its weight.
