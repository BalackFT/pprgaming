**Capcom keeps winning.** In results [published today](https://www.capcom.co.jp/ir/english/news/pdf/e261027b.pdf), Capcom reported net sales of **¥120.9 billion** (about $790 million) for April through September 2026, **up 49%** on last year. Operating profit rose **54.5% to ¥60.8 billion**. The company sold **36.86 million games** in six months, up from 23.85 million a year ago, and kept its full-year forecast unchanged.

**The numbers by game.** According to the title breakdown [reported by gamebiz](https://gamebiz.jp/news/434673):

- **Resident Evil Requiem** sold another 1.59 million copies in the half, taking it to **8.51 million** lifetime.
- **Pragmata**, the brand-new sci-fi IP that launched April 17, sold **2.73 million**. Only Resident Evil 4 remake sold more in the period.
- **Onimusha: Way of the Sword**, the first new Onimusha in 20 years, sold **1.78 million** since its September 4 launch.
- Older games did the heavy lifting: Resident Evil 4 remake sold 3.54 million (17.15 million lifetime), Resident Evil 2 remake sold 2.06 million, and Devil May Cry 5 sold 1.83 million.

**Catalog is king.** Back-catalog sales hit **32.18 million units**, a first-half record and about 87% of everything Capcom sold. Capcom credits Requiem for "driving growth in catalog sales": each new Resident Evil pulls players into the older games, helped by discounts. New releases sold 4.68 million units, up from 0.99 million a year ago, as [Famitsu also notes](https://www.famitsu.com/article/202610/92223). PC is now Capcom's biggest platform, with 21.03 million of 34.05 million digital units.

Capcom also pointed to the live-action Street Fighter movie, which opened worldwide on October 16, as part of its push to grow its brands outside games.

**PPR take:** While other studios are laying people off, Capcom's plan keeps paying off: release great games, keep the old ones cheap, and let one hit sell the rest of the series. A brand-new IP like Pragmata beating 2.7 million is the part we like best.
