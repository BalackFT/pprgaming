**Microsoft wants people to leave on their own.** In a [memo posted to the official Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/) on Monday, chief people officer Amy Coleman announced a new **Voluntary Exit Offer**: eligible employees can quit with a severance package and career help. About **60,000 people** are eligible, [according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html), which is roughly **27% of Microsoft's 223,000 staff**.

**Xbox is in.** Not every part of Microsoft is taking part, but sources told [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1018666/microsoft-expands-buyouts-60000-employees-eligible) that **Xbox** teams are on the list, along with Windows + Devices, Copilot, CoreAI, finance and HR. Marketing, security and commercial sales teams are not.

**How it works:**

- It's open to employees at level 70 and below (up to general managers and some vice presidents) in the **US, Canada, the UK and India**.
- Age, years of service and performance don't matter.
- The package matches Microsoft's standard severance, including tenure-based pay, continued stock vesting and healthcare for a set period, [per GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-makes-new-voluntary-exit-offer-to-more-than-a-quarter-of-its-workforce-including-some-execs/).
- Employees have until **early December** to decide, and those who accept leave on **December 31**.

**Context.** This is not a layoff, and Microsoft hasn't said how many people it expects to take the deal. But it follows **4,800 job cuts in July** that hit Xbox and sales hardest, and The Verge points to a Fortune report that Xbox CEO Asha Sharma is planning more layoff rounds. Earlier this year, more than 30% of the staff offered Microsoft's first voluntary retirement program took it.

**PPR take:** A buyout is kinder than a pink slip, but when a quarter of the company is invited to the door and Xbox is on the guest list, it's hard not to read it as a sign of more cuts to come. We'll be watching which Xbox studios lose people when the December deadline passes.
