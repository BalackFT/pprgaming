**Fortis Games is gone.** The San Francisco mobile publisher shut down on Tuesday, and [GamesBeat reports](https://gamesbeat.com/fortis-games-closes-down-affecting-440-jobs/) that as many as 440 people could lose their jobs. The news broke through a wave of LinkedIn posts from staff this afternoon. Communications director Scott Butterworth wrote that the company "has officially shuttered, abruptly putting more than 400 talented people out of work (myself included)."

The reason looks simple and harsh. Fortis was created and fully owned by casino giant Las Vegas Sands, and one production staffer, Gustavo Coronet, wrote that "Las Vegas Sands has chosen to end funding and part ways, terminating all of the contracts, worldwide." CEO Steven Chiang, a former EA Sports and WB Games executive, told GamesBeat the team is "disappointed we weren't able to achieve the goals we set for ourselves" and that the focus now is helping people find new jobs.

Fortis launched in 2022 with backing from Sands, which saw games as a way to diversify beyond gambling. It was co-founded by Chiang, Shawn Foust and Calvin Lau, and it started with about 150 people after buying three studios: Doppio Games in Portugal, Oktagon Games in Brazil and Metagame in Romania, [Mobilegamer.biz notes](https://mobilegamer.biz/fortis-games-appears-to-have-closed-with-over-400-staff-likely-affected/). The company's site lists three games, Mergemon Wilds, Twilight Towers and Bloom & Breakfast.

None of them really made it out. Twilight Towers soft-launched in September 2025, and on November 4 Fortis [announced it was shutting the game down](https://www.fortisgames.com/en-us/games/twilighttowers/news/sunset-announcement), pulling it from app stores and turning off purchases. Its servers go dark on November 16. Fortis said at the time, "we just weren't seeing strong enough player retention to make the game sustainable." [Insider Gaming adds](https://insider-gaming.com/fortis-games-shuts-down-440-jobs-expected-lost/) that Fortis itself hasn't made an official announcement about the closure and didn't respond to requests for comment.

**PPR take:** Four years, three studios on three continents, more than 400 people, and not a single game that got a full launch. That's what happens when one deep-pocketed owner is the whole plan and then decides it's done. Coronet says both projects had solid soft launch numbers, and we can't check that, but it doesn't matter much when the money walks away overnight. What gets me is how abrupt it sounds. Twilight Towers players got a farewell gift and a refund path last week. A lot of the people who built that game found out today, along with everyone in Portugal, Brazil and Romania. It's been a brutal year for game jobs, and it's only November. If you're a studio that hires, go look at Fortis LinkedIn profiles this week. There's a lot of talent about to be available.
