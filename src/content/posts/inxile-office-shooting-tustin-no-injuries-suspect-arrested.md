**Everyone at inXile Entertainment is safe, and that's the most important line in this story.** Shots were fired at the Tustin, California office building where the Xbox-owned studio works on Wednesday, November 11, and nobody was hurt. Police took a suspect into custody two days later. The news only reached most of us over the weekend, as [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/inxile-entertainment-shooting-xbox/) and [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/not-one-hurt-at-xbox-studio-inxile-entertainment-after-a-gunman-allegedly-opened-fire-on-the-building-2000756851) followed up on a first report from Aftermath.

## What police have said

Per Polygon, the shooting happened around 1:13 p.m. local time, and the Tustin Police Department described it on its official Facebook account as a drive-by. Kotaku, citing local news, says "several bullets" struck at least one building in the area, with five to eight shell casings left behind. Kotaku also understands the bullets hit the bottom floor of inXile's building, and that police at one point looked into whether the studio itself was the target.

Tustin police Sergeant Christopher Peterson told Aftermath that officers found multiple shell casings, processed the scene, and took a suspect into custody on Friday, November 13, "a little before 11." He said the investigation is "still kind of ongoing as to where and why." As of the latest reports, [police haven't named the suspect or given a motive](https://tbreak.com/inxile-office-shooting-tustin/), and it still isn't confirmed whether inXile was the intended target or simply in the line of fire. The building sits on an office campus called Flight at Tustin Legacy that houses other businesses too.

## inXile's response

The studio has kept it short. A spokesperson gave Aftermath and Kotaku the same statement: "We're relieved that no one was hurt at our inXile office. Our focus is on supporting our team, and we are working closely with law enforcement as they investigate."

inXile is the team behind the Wasteland series, and it's currently making Clockwork Revolution, the Xbox console exclusive that Kotaku notes is set for 2027. The studio hasn't said anything about changes to how its team is working while the investigation continues.
