**The PlayStation Plus app for PC has a month left.** Sony says it will retire the app on **December 10**, and it announced that in an unusual spot: the last few lines of [November's PlayStation Plus Game Catalog post](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/11/10/playstation-plus-game-catalog-for-november-death-stranding-2-on-the-beach-like-a-dragon-pirate-yakuza-in-hawaii-atomfall-dragon-quest-iii-hd-2d-remake-and-more/) on the PlayStation Blog, right under the Soul Reaver 2 and Freedom Wars Remastered listings.

The app let PlayStation Plus Premium members stream games from Sony's cloud catalog to a Windows PC. Sony's reason is simple. According to Adam Michel, who signed the post, "a very small number of eligible PlayStation Plus Premium members (less than 0.4%)" were using it, and dropping it lets Sony "focus on enhancing high-quality experiences that more of our members want to use." After December 10, the PlayStation Plus cloud streaming app on PC will no longer be available.

What doesn't change matters just as much. Sony says memberships and benefits on PS5 and PS4 aren't affected, and it will notify affected users directly. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/1031144/sony-playstation-plus-windows-pc-app-shutting-down) notes that Premium members can still stream games on a console or on PlayStation Portal. So the cloud catalog isn't going anywhere. The PC is just losing its way in.

The same post also lays out November's catalog: Death Stranding 2: On the Beach, Like a Dragon: Pirate Yakuza in Hawaii, Atomfall and Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake lead the Extra and Premium additions, with the full lineup playable from November 17 unless noted otherwise. A few titles, including Pirate Yakuza, land November 24 in the US and UK.

There's context around this, too. [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/playstation-plus-pc-app-is-being-shut-down-in-december-because-less-than-0-4-percent-of-eligible-subscribers-are-using-it/) and [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/PlayStation-Sony-is-discontinuing-its-PC-app-and-canceling-shooter-Fairgames-11520339.html) both point back to Bloomberg's report in March that Sony no longer plans to bring its big single-player games to PC. The app news landed the same day Sony [cancelled Fairgames](/news/playstation-cancels-fairgames-haven-studios-no-layoffs/), so it's been a busy Tuesday for PlayStation.

## The PPR take

Honestly, this one is hard to get upset about. Fewer than 0.4% of eligible members used it, and as PC Gamer points out, the catalog you could stream was mostly older PlayStation games, not new releases. If you were in that small group, December 10 is the date to remember, and the fallback is a console or a Portal, which isn't much help if your PC was the only screen you streamed to.

The bigger story is the direction. Pair this with the March report about single-player PC ports and it's hard not to read it as Sony leaning back into the console as the place to play its games. Sony hasn't said that in so many words, and it hasn't announced anything new about PC ports here, so we'll take the stated reason at face value. But if you're a PC player who was hoping PlayStation would keep opening up, this week didn't help. Keep an eye out for Sony's direct notice if you used the app.
