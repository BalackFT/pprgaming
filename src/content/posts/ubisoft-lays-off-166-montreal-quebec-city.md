**Another hard day for game developers.** Ubisoft is cutting 166 positions across its Montréal and Québec City operations, the company confirmed Thursday. [VGC reports](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/ubisoft-is-laying-off-166-staff-in-its-montreal-and-quebec-city-offices/) that's just under 4% of Ubisoft's entire Canadian workforce, and [Reuters](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Ubisoft+to+cut+166+jobs+in+Montr%C3%A9al+and+Qu%C3%A9bec+City/27374791.html) carried the same number from the company's statement.

## What Ubisoft said

"As our portfolio evolves, our production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs," a Ubisoft spokesperson said. The company added that it values "the contributions of these teams," and that those affected are getting severance, extended health care benefits, career transition services and other resources. [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2026/11/19/ubisoft-canada-quebec-layoffs-166-people) received the same statement.

Ubisoft hasn't said how the 166 cuts split between the two studios. VGC says it understands the cuts are mostly tied to projects that were canceled or rescoped. That's VGC's reporting, not something Ubisoft has confirmed.

## Two of Ubisoft's biggest studios

Ubisoft Montréal is the publisher's flagship, with around 4,000 people and a long history on Assassin's Creed, Far Cry, Watch Dogs and Rainbow Six. Ubisoft Québec has around 600 staff and led last year's Assassin's Creed Shadows, per MobileSyrup.

This is the latest round in Ubisoft's global cost-cutting plan. VGC counts at least six canceled games, seven postponed ones and several studio closures this year. In June, Ubisoft closed its Winnipeg and Belgrade studios and restructured Barcelona, with as many as 380 jobs lost combined, and last month came reports that it plans to shut RedLynx, the Trials studio, and downsize Ubisoft Singapore. It's also the second big layoff story we've covered this week, after [Firaxis cut about a third of its staff](/news/firaxis-layoffs-third-of-staff-studio-head-heather-hazen-steps-down/) on Tuesday.

**PPR take:** Rough news out of Québec. 166 is a number in a press release, but every one of those is a person who helped build games a lot of us have sunk hundreds of hours into, and they're getting this news a few weeks before the holidays. I'm glad Ubisoft is at least putting severance, extended health care and transition help on the table. That matters, and not every company does it. BUT this is round after round now, and "aligned with current production priorities" doesn't tell anyone much when the priorities keep moving. Canceled and rescoped projects aren't the fault of the folks who were building them. Montréal and Québec are full of seriously talented people, and I hope studios in the area are paying attention today. Thoughts are with everyone affected, and I hope you land somewhere that treats you right.
