**Unity Software has held recent talks to be acquired by a consortium that includes private equity firm Silver Lake Partners and Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund (PIF),** according to people familiar with the matter cited in a [Reuters exclusive](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/exclusive-silver-lake-pif-have-held-recent-talks-buy-gaming-software-provider-unity-sources-say-6529221) carried by [CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/exclusive-silver-lake-pif-have-held-recent-talks-buy-gaming-software-provider-unity-sources-say-6529221) and [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28236/silver-lake-and-saudi-pif-hold-talks-to-buy-unity-at-about-25bn). **No deal is certain**, and the plan and terms may still change.

## What’s on the table

Silver Lake, which already owns about **8%** of Unity, and PIF approached the company in recent weeks, the sources said. The buyer group discussed a valuation of about **$25 billion** before Unity’s latest earnings, which lifted its market capitalization to roughly **$21.5 billion**. Unity has been working with an investment bank to evaluate options after the approach.

Unity declined to comment. Silver Lake and PIF did not immediately respond to requests for comment, per Reuters.

[Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-unity-software-stock-surging-afterhours-today-93CH-5006123) reported Unity shares surged about **15%** in the regular session on earnings and rose about **6.9%** after hours following the Reuters report.

## Same buyers who took EA private

Silver Lake and PIF were part of the consortium — also including Affinity Partners — that took Electronic Arts private in a **$55 billion** deal that closed in August 2026. Unity’s engine and developer tools are already used by Scopely (*Monopoly Go!*) and Niantic (*Pokémon Go*), both owned by a group that includes PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners.

Silver Lake invested up to **$400 million** in Unity in 2017 and remains among its largest shareholders. Silver Lake’s Egon Durban sits on Unity’s board and would typically recuse himself from sale deliberations, Reuters noted.

## Earnings backdrop

Earlier Thursday, Unity reported third-quarter results that beat estimates: revenue rose **32%** to **$622.1 million**, adjusted earnings were **44 cents** per share, and it posted its **first quarterly GAAP profit** since going public in September 2020 — [CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/exclusive-silver-lake-pif-have-held-recent-talks-buy-gaming-software-provider-unity-sources-say-6529221) and Investing.com cite net income of **$122 million** (about a 20% margin). Investing.com also noted Vector AI ad-platform revenue grew **126%** year over year, with Q4 revenue guidance of **$683–691 million**.

Founded in Denmark in 2004 and based in San Francisco, Unity’s engine powers thousands of mobile, console and PC games, plus automotive, film, architecture and industrial uses.

**PPR take:** Big talk, not a done deal — and that distinction matters. Silver Lake and the Saudi PIF just helped take EA private for $55 billion, and now the same buyers are reportedly circling one of the biggest tools studios actually ship games with. If this closes, it would put a huge slice of the engine layer under the same ownership orbit as EA, Scopely and Niantic. That’s a lot of industry weight in a few hands. For now, all we have is sources saying talks happened, Unity evaluating options with a bank, and both sides staying quiet. We’re not going to guess what a take-private would mean for Unity’s pricing, runtime policy or the indie teams who rely on the engine every day. Those questions only get real if a deal is signed. Until then, treat this as a serious acquisition rumor with named buyers and a ballpark number — not a sale. We’ll keep watching and update when something firm lands.
