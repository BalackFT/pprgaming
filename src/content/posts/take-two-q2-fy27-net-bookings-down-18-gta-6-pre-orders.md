**Take-Two just had a soft quarter, and nobody there seems worried.** The GTA publisher [reported its fiscal second-quarter results](https://ir.take2games.com/static-files/a43ab8e3-c03a-4b7e-a6a8-f3cd2485e2df) on November 9 for the three months ending September 30. Net bookings fell 18% to $1.60 billion, down from $1.96 billion a year ago, and GAAP net revenue slipped to $1.50 billion from $1.77 billion. Recurrent spending, the in-game purchases and add-ons that make up most of its business, dropped 9% and still accounted for 81% of bookings. Take-Two posted a $54 million net loss, narrower than last year's $133.9 million.

The miss mostly comes down to basketball. NBA 2K27 has sold more than 4 million units since its September 4 launch and had the second-best launch bookings in the series' history, but it still came in under Take-Two's forecast, [GamesIndustry.biz reported](https://gamedev.net/news/take-two-q2-net-bookings-fall-18-to-16bn-as-nba-2k27-misses-forecast-but-gta-6-pre-orders-are-tracking-far-in-excess-of-expectations-r7023/). The company blamed an unusually crowded release window and expects post-launch content to help.

Then there's the elephant in Vice City. "The launch of Grand Theft Auto VI is ten days away and, with pre-orders continuing to reach unprecedented levels, we expect to deliver an historic year for our company," CEO Strauss Zelnick said. Take-Two says GTA 6 pre-orders are tracking "far in excess" of its initial expectations, and on the analyst call executives said Rockstar has finished the game, per [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/all-the-takeaways-on-gta-from-the-take-two-earnings-call-strauss-zelnick/). GTA 5, for the record, is now at nearly 235 million units.

Here's the odd part. Take-Two kept its full-year outlook at $8.0 to $8.2 billion in net bookings rather than raising it. It is guiding $3.35 to $3.5 billion for the October to December quarter, which covers roughly six weeks of GTA 6 sales. Zelnick told [GamesIndustry.biz](https://gamebastion.com/n/there-s-no-such-thing-as-easy-money-in-our/524751) the company is holding guidance because "the release is only 10 days away and we'll know a lot more after the release." On the call he was even more cautious: "it makes me nervous even saying those words... we still aren't ready to declare victory." Investors didn't share the nerves. The stock rose about 5% after hours, [GamesBeat noted](https://gamesbeat.com/take-two-reports-bookings-for-fiscal-q2-of-1-6b-down-18-and-below-expectations/). Take-Two also expects operating expenses to climb about 21% next quarter, mostly from GTA 6 marketing.

GTA VI launches November 19 on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Pre-loads are already rolling out, as [we covered yesterday](/news/gta-6-pre-load-tonight-212gb-multiplayer-files-the-festival-miami/).

**PPR take:** An 18% drop would be a headline at most publishers. At Take-Two it's a rounding error before the biggest launch in years. For players, the useful bits are that Rockstar has finished the game, the date is holding, and pre-order demand is real. I wouldn't bet on another delay at this point. Holding guidance flat is classic Zelnick caution, and it leaves Take-Two plenty of room to beat its own number once the sales come in. The NBA 2K27 note is worth watching, though. A franchise that sells 4 million copies and still "misses" shows how high the bar is for annual sports games, and how much pressure post-launch monetization carries. Ten more days.
