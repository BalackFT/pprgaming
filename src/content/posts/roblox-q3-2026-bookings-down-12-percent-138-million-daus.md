**Down, but not as far as expected.** Roblox [reported its third-quarter results](https://ir.roblox.com/news/news-details/2026/Roblox-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx) on Thursday. According to its [shareholder letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm), bookings came in at **$1.7 billion**, down 12% from a year ago, but above the high end of the company's own guidance. Roblox says the drop reflects a comparison with the "extraordinary growth" it saw in 2025, and that bookings are still up more than 50% over two years.

**The numbers**

- **Revenue:** $1.6 billion, up 16% year over year
- **Bookings:** $1.7 billion, down 12% year over year
- **Daily active users:** 138 million, up 12% from the previous quarter
- **Hours engaged:** nearly 34 billion, up 17% from the previous quarter
- **Net loss:** $230 million, compared with $258 million a year earlier
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** $138 million, up from $46 million

**Players 18 and over** made up 29% of global age-checked daily users and 37% in the U.S., up from 27% and 33% in the second quarter. Roblox calls that its fastest-growing group and says U.S. adult users spend more than 50% more than younger ones.

**AI is a big part of the pitch.** Roblox says more than 80% of its top 100 games used AI tools over the past month, and two games built "almost entirely with AI," *Break and Steal an Egg* and *Huss Valley*, broke into its top 20. Its new prompt-to-game tool, **Build**, is in alpha in New Zealand, Singapore, Serbia and Thailand, where more than 500,000 people have used it and published over 70,000 games.

The company also laid out two new initiatives. **Roblox Everywhere** would let creators self-publish standalone Roblox games to the app store of their choice, with a first phase planned for the first half of next year using select top games. **The Universal Player** includes a goal of using AI to import existing console, PC and mobile games onto Roblox.

**Looking ahead:** for the current quarter, Roblox expects revenue growth of 4% to 12% ($1.47 billion to $1.58 billion) and bookings **down 10% to 15%** ($1.89 billion to $2.0 billion). It says that assumes the usual seasonal dip in daily users between Q3 and Q4, and it's using a wider range than usual because so much of its holiday bookings land in the last week of December.

**PPR take:** The headline drop is mostly last year's numbers being huge. The more interesting part is how hard Roblox is leaning on AI-built games and grown-up players to drive what comes next.
