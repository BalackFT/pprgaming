**The man in charge of GTA 6's publisher has a message for the AI industry, and he isn't being subtle about it.** Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick told [IGN](https://sea.ign.com/grand-theft-auto-vi-rumored-title/251724/take-two-enhancing-technical-defenses-and-legal-activities-to-combat-gta-6-ai-reverse-engineering-th) that companies like OpenAI and Anthropic should be guarding other people's intellectual property as hard as they guard their own. [GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/grand-theft-auto/ahead-of-gta-6-take-two-ceo-calls-out-anthropic-and-openai-over-ai-game-clones-as-its-ironic-to-stomp-on-someone-elses-ip/) picked the comments up today, five days before Grand Theft Auto VI hits PS5 and Xbox Series X|S on November 19.

## What he said

IGN asked whether Take-Two would like other AI companies to follow Anthropic, which recently changed Claude to steer it away from recreating existing games. Zelnick's answer: "I think all companies should be protecting intellectual property, their own and everyone else's. And if you're in the intellectual property business, which Anthropic is and OpenAI is, then it seems ironic on the one hand to stomp on someone else's IP and hope that yours is protected."

The Claude change he was asked about is real and fairly narrow. As [GamesRadar+ reported](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/amid-steams-battle-with-ai-clones-claude-stops-users-from-recreating-games-using-its-controversial-tech-to-prevent-ip-infringement/), Anthropic's Addy Osmani said the company added two sentences to Claude Code's prompt, because "using Claude to infringe IP is against our usage policy." Original games and genre games are still fair game. GamesRadar+ notes OpenAI doesn't appear to have a similar rule yet.

## Why Take-Two cares right now

The worry isn't just copycat indie games on Steam. IGN lays out the fear that AI-assisted decompiling, plus the ongoing back-and-forth over PS5 jailbreaks, could put an unofficial version of GTA 6 on PC long before Rockstar ships one. GTA 5 was already briefly made playable in a browser last month before it was pulled.

Zelnick told IGN he sees it as "a creative matter," not a financial threat. In the investor Q&A after Take-Two's latest earnings, he went further, admitting the industry's natural defenses (huge file sizes, server connections) won't hold forever: "We are utterly focused on enhancing our technical defenses and enhancing our legal activities appropriately." He also admitted piracy "is likely to invade our business to a greater or lesser extent," but called it "manageable."

**PPR take:** Zelnick's got a point, and it's a point a lot of smaller devs have been making for months with much less of a megaphone. When a solo developer pulls a Steam demo because they're scared it'll get cloned and sold before launch, something's broken. Anthropic putting a guardrail in is a start. It's a prompt rule, though, not a wall, and it only works if the other big players do the same. Now, let's be real about the other half of this. Take-Two isn't saying this out of the goodness of its heart. The single most expensive game ever made launches Thursday, and "enhancing our legal activities" is CEO-speak for a lot of cease-and-desist letters coming soon. Both things can be true. I'd just like that same energy for the little studios that don't have Rockstar's legal team on speed dial.
