**Cloud gaming, by the hour.** Microsoft has started selling **Cloud Playtime** to Xbox Insiders who don't have Game Pass, letting them buy blocks of streaming hours for games they already own. It's a preview ahead of a broader rollout in **November**, according to [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/10/21/cloud-playtime-now-available-to-test-with-xbox-insiders/).

**The introductory prices:**

- **10 hours:** $3.99
- **25 hours:** $7.99
- **50 hours:** $11.99

Microsoft says the packs are ad-free, with shorter wait times and higher-quality streams, and purchased hours stay on your account until you use them. You still need to own a cloud-playable game (or pick an eligible free-to-play one) and a supported device, as [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/1015321/xbox-cloud-gaming-pay-as-you-go-cloud-hours-pricing) notes. Insiders can also keep testing free, ad-supported streaming, which is capped at one-hour sessions.

The packs matter for subscribers too. [Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/xbox-cloud-gaming-playtime-pack-prices/) reports Game Pass cloud time is moving to monthly caps (15 hours on Ultimate, 10 on Premium, 5 on Essential), with these packs as the top-up once you run out. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2026/10/xbox-wants-you-to-pay-24-to-40-cents-per-hour-to-stream-games-you-already-bought/) worked it out to about 24 to 40 cents an hour, on top of buying the game.

**PPR take:** Hours that never expire are a fair deal for occasional streamers. Paying again to stream a game you already bought will be a tough sell for everyone else.
