**Garrett is coming back on December 3.** Nightdive Studios, Eidos-Montréal and Atari [announced today](https://www.gamespress.com/Nightdive-Studios-Eidos-Montreal-And-Ataris-Thief-The-Dark-Project-Rem) that Thief: The Dark Project Remastered launches that day on PS5, Xbox Series X|S (with Xbox Play Anywhere), Switch 2, PS4, Switch, and PC through Steam, Epic Games Store and GOG. Pre-orders are open now.

## What's in the package

The remaster is built on Nightdive's KEX Engine and covers Looking Glass Studios' 1998 original plus everything from the 1999 Thief Gold re-release, which added three missions and five new enemy types. Per [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/11/thief-the-dark-project-remastered-launches-december-3), the upgrades include improved textures, models and animations, up to 4K at 120 frames per second, modern controller support with a weapon and item wheel, gyro aiming, and a mission selector so you can replay any level you've already finished. There's also a behind-the-scenes Vault and "small fixes to the original level design."

The big surprise is **The Tarnished Mirror**, an all-new, self-contained campaign that ships with the game. It's made by the creators of Thief: The Black Parade, the huge fan-made expansion, and the press release says it's meant to bridge The Dark Project and Thief II: The Metal Age. It starts as a simple theft and turns into imprisonment, uneasy alliances and a trip into a drowned, Lovecraftian necropolis. The remaster also supports player-made missions, The Black Parade included.

## Price and editions

The Standard Edition is **$29.99** and the Digital Deluxe Edition is **$34.99**, adding an art book, the soundtrack and an excerpt from an upcoming Thief novelization. On PS5 and Switch 2 there are physical Standard ($29.99), Steelbook ($39.99) and Deluxe Trapezoid ($69.99) editions. That last one recreates the original 1998 trapezoid big box. Switch owners get the Switch 2 Edition upgrade for free. Full details are on [Atari's product page](https://atari.com/products/thief-the-dark-project-remastered).

**PPR take:** This is the right way to bring back a classic. Thief basically invented first-person stealth built on light and sound, and its levels still hold up, so the job here was to make it run and control like a modern game without sanding off what made it great. Nightdive has a strong record on that. What really caught my eye, though, is The Tarnished Mirror. Bringing The Black Parade team in and making their work official canon is a big deal, and it's a nice nod to a fan community that kept Thief alive for more than 25 years. Mod support at launch is a smart call too. At $29.99 with a new campaign included, this one's an easy pick for stealth fans, and a great way to finally see what all the fuss was about if you missed it in 1998. We'll see how it plays on December 3.
